# Notch messaging

> **Descriptor:** Evidence-led attention markets  
> **Primary line:** Where attention turns into evidence.  
> **Status:** Planning / not yet deployed

This document turns the non-visual parts of the Notch scope into usable product language. It intentionally excludes logo, colour, typography, imagery, motion and art-direction guidance.

## Strategic foundation

### What Notch is

Notch is a NEAR testnet app for taking bounded positions on measurable internet attention, with source-linked research and verifiable settlement evidence.

### Who it is for

The priority user sees a topic gaining attention and wants to understand the source, make a defined call and check the result later. Secondary users include research-minded participants, community organizers and NEAR ecosystem evaluators.

### The job

> Help me turn a noisy trend into a clear question, a bounded choice and an evidence-backed result.

### The problem

Attention moves quickly, but most people cannot see what is being measured, how a market will resolve or whether an explanation is separate from the payout decision.

### The promise

Notch lets people read the rule, take a bounded testnet position and inspect the evidence behind the result.

### The difference

Notch freezes the source, observation window, formula and market rule before entry. It uses AI to explain public evidence, but never to control settlement.

### The reason to believe

The target architecture includes a shared NEAR contract, NEP-141 test-token collateral, deterministic resolution, canonical evidence JSON, an evidence digest and explorer-verifiable receipts.

### Strategic enemy

Noise without measurement, markets without receipts and AI narration treated as financial authority.

## Messaging architecture

### Core message

> **Notch turns measurable internet attention into clear, bounded markets with proof.**

### Messaging house

#### Roof

**Where attention turns into evidence.**

#### Pillar 1: Clear questions

Notch turns a vague trend into a market question with a side, a time window, a source and a rule.

Proof language:

- frozen question;
- named source;
- baseline and target window;
- threshold or battle rule;
- invalidation policy.

#### Pillar 2: Bounded participation

Users choose UP or DOWN, or one side of a Trend Battle, with test collateral in a pooled binary market. The hackathon core excludes leverage, liquidation and live customer funds.

Proof language:

- NEAR testnet wallet;
- NEP-141 test token;
- shared escrow;
- close time;
- claim or refund path.

#### Pillar 3: Evidence after the result

Notch shows the observations, formula, result, digest and transaction references. The receipt explains what happened instead of asking the user to trust a chart.

Proof language:

- canonical evidence JSON;
- versioned formula;
- source response hash;
- onchain evidence hash;
- explorer link.

## Pitches

### Elevator pitch

People can see attention moving online, but most tools either show noise or hide the rules behind a prediction market. Notch makes the question explicit. It uses a named source, a frozen window and a deterministic rule, then lets users take a fully collateralized testnet position and inspect the evidence after resolution. NEAR supplies the wallet, contract and settlement layer. AI provides context, not payout authority.

### Thirty-second pitch

Notch is an evidence-led attention market on NEAR testnet. A user opens a topic, reads the source and rule, chooses UP or DOWN or picks a side in a Trend Battle, then waits for the defined observation window. When the data is available, the resolver publishes the calculation and receipt. The user can inspect the evidence and claim or refund. The point is simple: attention becomes a market only when the rule and result can be checked.

### Judge-facing pitch

Notch demonstrates two real testnet market modes on one NEAR contract. It funds positions with test collateral, resolves them from a versioned public-data rule and preserves the evidence digest and transaction trail. A separate Intents-compatible funding flow shows the ecosystem path without pretending that a community testnet sandbox is official mainnet 1Click.

## Feature-to-value language

| Feature | Say what it does | Say why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Frozen source and window | The market fixes the source and observation window before entry. | Everyone knows what the result will use. |
| Deterministic resolver | The resolver applies a versioned rule to the recorded data. | The result can be reproduced. |
| Evidence receipt | The receipt links source, observations, formula, result and transaction. | You can inspect why the market resolved that way. |
| Pooled binary settlement | The market uses simple fully collateralized positions. | You can understand the commitment without leverage. |
| NEAR testnet contract | Positions, claims and refunds use real testnet contract state. | The integration is an action a judge can verify. |
| NEAR AI brief | The brief summarizes cited public context and uncertainty. | The research is easier to read without giving AI payout authority. |
| Private research | Watchlists and notes are owner-scoped offchain. | You can investigate before sharing a hypothesis. |

## Verbal identity

### Voice

Notch sounds like a careful market editor who knows the difference between a signal, an interpretation and a result.

### Voice rules

#### Be exact before being exciting

State the metric, rule or status before adding energy.

- Use: "UP means the target day's pageviews grew at least 15% against the frozen baseline."
- Avoid: "Bet on the next big thing."

#### Be curious, not prophetic

Invite inspection instead of promising a forecast will be right.

- Use: "What moved, and what does the source actually show?"
- Avoid: "Our model knows what happens next."

#### Be calm at the money boundary

Use plain status language around funding, positions, claims and refunds.

- Use: "Your test-token deposit is pending. Do not send it again."
- Avoid: "You're all set" while destination delivery is unverified.

#### Keep the caveat next to the claim

Do not hide a material limitation in a footnote.

- Use: "Replay market. Uses previously published data, not a live forecast."
- Avoid: "Trade the live attention market" for a replay flow.

### Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page | Clear and inviting | "Read the signal before you take a side." |
| Market page | Exact and neutral | "The rule is fixed before the first position." |
| Research brief | Curious and qualified | "The source shows a rise in pageviews. It does not establish why." |
| Funding | Calm and procedural | "Provider acknowledged the deposit. We are checking destination delivery." |
| Resolution | Factual and inspectable | "UP resolved at 18.4% growth against a 15% threshold." |
| Error | Direct and helpful | "The source is unavailable. No result has been recorded." |
| Demo | Confident and honest | "This is a testnet replay using previously published observations." |

### Cadence

Use short sentences for state changes. Use one idea per paragraph. Put the action at the end of a sentence when the user needs to act.

### Preferred vocabulary

`mark`, `signal`, `source`, `rule`, `window`, `baseline`, `side`, `pool`, `evidence`, `receipt`, `proof`, `resolve`, `claim`, `refund`, `replay`, `testnet`, `inspect`, `compare`.

### Restricted vocabulary

Avoid or qualify `revolutionary`, `seamless`, `next-generation`, `AI-powered`, `alpha`, `guaranteed`, `risk-free`, `anonymous`, `instant`, `odds`, `wager`, `jackpot`, `moon`, `yield`, `autonomous`, `official` and `real-time`.

## Product language and UX copy

### Landing page

**Headline:** Where attention turns into evidence.

**Body:** Notch turns a measurable internet-interest question into a clear testnet market. Read the source, choose a side and check the receipt when the window closes.

**Trust line:** Testnet only. Source and settlement rules are shown before entry.

### Market card

- `Trend Trading`
- `Trend Battle`
- `Closes [date/time]`
- `Source: Wikimedia pageviews`
- `Rule: [plain-language rule]`
- `Replay market` when historical data is used

### Market detail

Use this order:

1. Question
2. Source and window
3. Rule and baseline
4. Current pool split
5. Research brief
6. Risk and boundary note
7. Entry action

### Entry confirmation

> You are entering [UP / DOWN / topic side] with [amount] test USDC. This market uses pooled settlement. The source, window and rule are fixed. Confirm only if you understand the testnet boundary.

### Waiting state

> Awaiting data. The observation is not available yet. No result has been invented.

### Resolution state

> Result recorded. Review the calculation and evidence receipt before claiming.

### Claim state

> Claim available. The contract recorded your eligibility. Confirm the testnet transaction to receive the payout.

### Refund state

> Market void. The stated resolution condition could not be met safely. Claim your original contribution back.

### Funding state language

- `Quote ready`: Review the expected receipt and expiry. Testnet only.
- `Pending deposit`: The provider has not confirmed delivery. Do not send the deposit again.
- `Processing`: We are checking the provider status. Keep this window open or return later.
- `Success`: Destination test-token balance confirmed. You can enter a market.
- `Failed or refunded`: The funding path did not complete. Review the provider status before retrying.

Never collapse provider acknowledgement, destination delivery and market entry into one `funded` label.

### AI brief language

Use three labels:

- **Observed:** what the cited source reports.
- **Possible drivers:** interpretations supported by the supplied sources.
- **Not established:** what the data does not prove.

The brief must say when it uses a replay, when a source is delayed and when a context API is unavailable.

## Objection handling

**Is this real-money trading?**  
No. The hackathon build uses valueless NEAR testnet tokens. A mainnet product would need legal, security and market-integrity review.

**Does AI decide the result?**  
No. AI may summarize cited public sources. A deterministic resolver applies the precommitted rule.

**Is it anonymous?**  
No. Public chain activity remains public. Private research is an access-controlled offchain feature. Commit-reveal can hide a choice before reveal, not necessarily the wallet or amount.

**Is the funding flow official NEAR Intents?**  
The target design separates the official mainnet read-only 1Click quote from the Tidepool community testnet sandbox. They have different labels and trust boundaries.

**Does a higher pageview count prove organic interest?**  
No. It proves only that the named source metric changed under the stated rule.

## Claim boundaries

| Status | Safe wording |
|---|---|
| Target | "Notch will support..." |
| Demo | "This replay uses previously published observations..." |
| Roadmap | "Planned after the hackathon..." |
| Hypothesis | "We expect..." |
| Verified shipped result | Name the exact test, transaction, source or deployment evidence. |

Do not use `live`, `private`, `instant`, `anonymous`, `official Intents swap`, `real adoption`, `organic attention` or `completed` unless the exact claim has current evidence.

## Competition and implementation story

### Primary fit

**Unstoppable DeFi:** a real NEAR contract, test-token collateral, positions, settlement, claims and a separate Intents-compatible funding demonstration.

### Secondary fit

**Open Innovation:** a new use of public attention data, consumer markets and explainable research.

These are strategic fit assessments. Recheck the live rules and submission schema before submitting.

### Demo story

1. Attention moves, but the rules are usually unclear.
2. Notch defines a measurable question before entry.
3. A user connects a NEAR testnet wallet and chooses a side.
4. A resolver applies the named source and versioned rule.
5. The user inspects the receipt and claims or refunds.
6. The funding path and privacy limits are shown honestly.

### Build priority language

- **P0:** both modes, shared contract, test collateral, deterministic resolution, evidence receipts and a verifiable funding demonstration.
- **P1:** market pages, AI briefs, Portfolio, share cards and safe private research.
- **P2:** commit-reveal only if its accounting, secret loss and non-reveal behavior are tested.
- **Roadmap:** secondary trading, multi-source index, selective disclosure, confidential funding and mainnet operation.

### Acceptance criteria

- At least one enter, close, resolve and claim flow for each mode.
- No double claims, negative balances or excess payouts in automated tests.
- A source outage produces Awaiting Data or Void, not a guessed result.
- Provider failure is shown as failure or refund, not success.
- AI failure does not break trading.
- Mobile wallet and transaction states remain understandable.
- The public README and demo match the checked-out implementation.

## Messaging tests

Before release, test whether a stranger can answer these questions in five seconds:

1. What is Notch?
2. What is being measured?
3. What can I do here?
4. What happens to my test tokens?
5. How do I know the result is fair?
6. Which parts are live, replayed or planned?

Test the headline, descriptor and first market card separately. A name can be memorable while the category remains unclear. Fix the category language, not the name, when that happens.
